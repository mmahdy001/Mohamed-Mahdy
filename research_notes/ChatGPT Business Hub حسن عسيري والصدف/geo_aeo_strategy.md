# GEO/AEO & AI Visibility Strategy for an Artist and a Production Company (2025-2026)

## What are the proven/recommended GEO/AEO tactics for 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
There is no single "silver bullet" tactic with hard causal proof. The most consistently-recurring, lowest-risk tactics across independent-ish sources are: (1) earned media / third-party brand mentions (stronger signal than raw backlinks), (2) consistent Organization/Person/MusicGroup structured data plus a `sameAs` entity-consistency program tying the brand to Wikidata/LinkedIn/official profiles, (3) answer-first, FAQ-structured content, and (4) building genuine topical/entity authority over 6-12 months. `llms.txt` is a popular but *unproven* tactic — adoption is rising fast while measured citation impact is flat to negative. Nearly all specific "X% uplift" statistics in this space come from vendors selling GEO services and should be treated as directional, not verified.

### Cited Findings
- SEO/AEO/GEO framing: SEO = technical health/content/domain authority (foundation); AEO = optimizing for direct-answer formats; GEO = getting AI models across platforms to recognize, trust, and recommend a brand — [bol-agency.com](https://www.bol-agency.com/blog/what-is-geo-and-aeo-how-ai-is-changing-b2b-seo)
- Recurring tactics across multiple 2026 guides: schema markup (Article/FAQ types), answer-first content structure, original/proprietary research as more citable than listicles (vendor claim, unverified), and measurement shifting from traffic to "citation frequency" and "share of voice" across ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini/Copilot/Claude — [lumar.io](https://lumar.io/blog/best-practice/geo-aeo-seo-experts-weigh-in-on-ai-search), [superlines.io](https://superlines.io/articles/generative-engine-optimization-geo-guide)
- One guide estimates 6-12 months of consistent content development before significant citation gains — [lumar.io](https://lumar.io/blog/best-practice/geo-aeo-seo-experts-weigh-in-on-ai-search)
- Survey data (vendor-sourced, unverified methodology): 47% of brands still lack a GEO strategy; ~64% plan to upskill existing SEO/marketing staff to do GEO internally — [martech.org](https://martech.org/the-competition-for-brand-visibility-has-moved-to-ai-search/)
- **llms.txt adoption vs. effectiveness**: Adoption estimates vary (8.7% of top 1,000 sites per Rankability mid-2026; ~10.13% of 300k domains per SE Ranking; only 7.4% of Fortune 500 as of March 2026) — [rankability.com](https://www.rankability.com/data/llms-txt-adoption/), [somethinginc.com](https://somethinginc.com/blog/does-llms-txt-work-2026-data/). SE Ranking's correlation analysis found llms.txt presence did **not** predict AI citations. Ahrefs found that of 137,000 monitored domains, 28% had an llms.txt file and **97% of those received zero requests** in May 2026 — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/), [letsdatascience.com](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ahrefs-finds-97-of-llmstxt-files-receive-no-requests-fc471b83). Google has stated llms.txt "won't negatively or positively impact your visibility or rankings" in Search. As of Q1 2026, none of OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, or Perplexity have publicly confirmed their production systems read/act on it.
- **Digital PR / earned media is a stronger correlate than backlinks**: Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands (2025) found branded web mentions correlate with Google AI Overview visibility at 0.664, vs. 0.218 for raw backlinks — [prosperitymedia.com.au](https://prosperitymedia.com.au/digital-pr-statistics-2026-what-the-data-shows-about-seo-geo-and-ai-search/)
- Muck Rack (July 2025): >95% of cited links in AI responses came from non-paid sources, of which ~85% were earned media — [natlawreview.com](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/muck-rack-study-generative-ai-relies-heavily-earned-media-and-journalism)
- Press-release wire citations (PR Newswire, Business Wire, GlobeNewswire) grew roughly 5x between July-December 2025, rising from 1.2% to 6% of citation share — [journalofcyberpolicy.com](https://journalofcyberpolicy.com/?p=346639) (secondary report of Muck Rack/Link Build data; treat as directional)
- Only ~11% of SEO teams (Link Build 2025 report) vs. ~40% (BuzzStream 2026 Digital PR survey) report having a *repeatable* process for earning AI-citations — figures conflict across surveys — [journalofcyberpolicy.com](https://journalofcyberpolicy.com/?p=346639)
- **Structured data evidence is mixed/contested**: One analysis (AccuraCast, ~9,000 citation sources, Sept 2025) found Person schema present on 56% of cited sources, highest of any schema type — [topicintelligence.ai](https://topicintelligence.ai/?p=1358). Counter-evidence: Search Atlas reported analyzing 5.5M AI responses and found **no measurable link** between schema coverage and citation rates on OpenAI, Gemini, or Perplexity — [radiantelephant.com](https://www.radiantelephant.com/?p=51466). Google's Search team (April 2025) and Bing's Fabrice Canel (March 2025) both said structured data "helps" search/Copilot understanding, but neither claimed it directly earns citations.
- Entity consistency (`sameAs` + matching legal name/logo/founding facts/category across Wikidata, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, official site) is widely recommended as the mechanism that lets AI engines merge mentions into one confident entity, but evidence specific to AI engines (vs. traditional search) is thin and mostly vendor-sourced — [gogochimp.com](https://www.gogochimp.com/blog/entity-seo-brand-mentions), [tenten.co](https://geo.tenten.co/en/blog/brand-entity-consistency-checklist)
- Review presence matters for local/business queries: a Trustpilot analysis (secondary citation, unverified) of 800,000 AI-generated answers found businesses with little/no review presence rarely appeared in AI recommendations — [sociusmarketing.com context](https://www.sociusmarketing.com/?p=5140) (cited via secondary blog, original not verified)

### Inferences
- Because structured-data evidence is contested while earned-media/entity-mention evidence is comparatively stronger and consistent across independent studies (Ahrefs, Muck Rack), **PR and third-party coverage should be the higher-priority investment** relative to schema markup, though schema is cheap/low-risk and should still be implemented as table-stakes hygiene, not as the primary growth lever.
- `llms.txt` should be treated as a "nice to have, low cost, unproven ROI" item — implement if cheap, but do not expect it to move AI citation outcomes in 2025-2026, and do not substitute it for actual content/PR work.
- The field is young enough that most "verified" statistics are self-reported by vendors selling GEO/AEO services (Kalicube, BlitzMetrics, AccuraCast-adjacent blogs, Yext, Uberall, etc.) — treat specific percentages as *directional signals* about where effort should go, not as guaranteed outcomes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or platform-published (OpenAI/Google/Anthropic/Perplexity) study quantifies the ROI of any single GEO tactic in isolation.
- No data found specifically isolating effects for individual artists/musicians vs. B2B companies — most large studies are general-business or B2B SaaS focused.

## How do AI assistants source information about a person/company, and what matters for a Saudi/Arabic entity?

### Takeaway
ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini each blend live web retrieval (via search indexes / "grounding") with pretrained knowledge, but the mix and the primary source types differ by platform: directories/listings and business websites dominate for local/business queries, while earned media, Knowledge Graph data, and brand-owned sites dominate for entity/person queries. For Arabic/Saudi/MENA entities specifically, there is a well-documented "Arabic AI visibility gap" — Arabic-language content and Arabic Wikipedia coverage are thinner than English equivalents, which structurally disadvantages Arabic-only entities in AI answers.

### Cited Findings
- **ChatGPT Search mechanics (official + secondary)**: OpenAI's official launch post says ChatGPT's search model is a fine-tuned GPT-4o variant trained partly on data distilled from o1-preview — [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-search/). OpenAI's Help Center confirms inline citations can be hovered/clicked to see sources — [help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10093903). Secondary (unverified by OpenAI directly) reporting says ChatGPT Search sometimes works with third-party search providers and rewrites prompts into targeted queries before retrieval — [geotoolbox.ai](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-chatgpt-search-works). ChatGPT Search became available to all users with no signup on February 5, 2025.
- Without a triggered web search, ChatGPT's baseline knowledge reflects its training cutoff, not live data (one guide notes the pre-search-era cutoff was pre-October 2023; specifics vary by model version) — [surmado.com context, via WebSearch synthesis]
- **Directories/listings and websites for local/business queries**: Insites audit of 10,000 US local businesses found directory/listing platforms appeared in 35-39% of ChatGPT-style queries, the second most common source type — [insites.com](https://insites.com/blog/where-does-chatgpt-get-its-information-about-local-businesses/). BrightLocal's study (Dec 2024 data point) found ChatGPT used business websites as a source 58% of the time; Instagram was cited by both Google AI Mode and Perplexity — [brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)
- **Gemini sourcing**: Yext study found 52.15% of Gemini citations came from brand-owned websites; Gemini is described as benefiting heavily from Google's Knowledge Graph and "verified sources" — [yext.com](https://www.yext.com/blog/ai-visibility-in-2025-how-gemini-chatgpt-perplexity-cite-brands). A citation-accuracy test (Talkory) found Gemini with Search Grounding enabled achieves an 84% valid-URL rate and 80% source-supports-claim rate — [talkory.ai](https://www.talkory.ai/blog/chatgpt-vs-perplexity-vs-gemini-ai-citations-accuracy) (single vendor test, not independently replicated)
- **Perplexity**: Built around live-web retrieval with inline citations by design; several vendors describe it as the most source-transparent of the major assistants — [yext.com](https://www.yext.com/blog/ai-visibility-in-2025-how-gemini-chatgpt-perplexity-cite-brands)
- **Arabic/MENA gap**: One MENA-focused analysis describes "near-invisibility of Arabic websites and brands in AI-generated responses" as a key regional problem; another notes Arabic-language content has weaker AI indexing than English — [found via WebSearch synthesis of MENA sources, no single primary study]. Arabic Wikipedia content is generally less comprehensive than the English equivalent for the same topics — [menastartupdigest.com](https://menastartupdigest.com/wikipedia-reports-significant-traffic-drop-amid-rise-of-ai-search-and-social-video/) (secondary reporting)
- Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) — the register used in formal publishing and by Arabic Wikipedia — is understood across all GCC nationalities, making MSA content (vs. dialect-only content) the safer choice for broad regional AI visibility — [cited via WebSearch synthesis, multiple MENA GEO vendor sources, no single authoritative citation found]
- Wikimedia Foundation reported an 8% year-over-year decline in human pageviews, attributed in part to AI search answers substituting for direct Wikipedia visits — [menastartupdigest.com](https://menastartupdigest.com/wikipedia-reports-significant-traffic-drop-amid-rise-of-ai-search-and-social-video/)
- Wikimedia Foundation has signed data-licensing deals with AI companies including Meta and Microsoft — [menastartupdigest.com](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=26213) (relevant because it suggests Wikipedia/Wikidata content is being directly ingested into some AI training/grounding pipelines)

### Inferences
- Because ChatGPT and Perplexity lean on business websites, directories, and earned-media mentions more than on any single "verified profile" feature, a Saudi/Arabic entity's own website plus Arabic-language press coverage likely matters more for AI visibility than any platform-specific business directory listing.
- Because Gemini leans more on Google's Knowledge Graph and brand-owned sites, a strong Google Knowledge Panel + Wikidata entry is likely more impactful specifically for Gemini/Google AI Mode than for ChatGPT or Perplexity.
- Given the documented Arabic content gap, bilingual (Arabic + English) coverage — especially in MSA — is likely necessary to be surfaced accurately in both Arabic-language queries and English-language queries about a Saudi entity.

### Gaps
- No official OpenAI, Google, or Perplexity statement specifically describes how Arabic-language or Saudi/GCC queries are handled differently (if at all) from English queries.
- No controlled/independent study (vs. vendor self-reported) quantifying the "Arabic AI visibility gap" with hard numbers was found.

## What is the real relationship between Google Business Profile and how ChatGPT/other assistants answer business queries?

### Takeaway
There is solid evidence that Google's *own* AI products (Gemini-powered Ask Maps, AI Overviews) draw directly on Google Business Profile (GBP) data, but claims that ChatGPT directly ingests or is "confirmed" to use GBP data are repeated across marketing blogs without an official OpenAI citation. Treat the GBP-to-ChatGPT link as plausible/indirect (via GBP data surfacing on the web, in directories, or in Bing's index) rather than a confirmed direct pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- Google launched "Ask Maps," a conversational AI layer powered by Gemini, in March 2026, answering local queries by pulling from Google Business Profiles and reviews — [localogy.com](https://www.localogy.com/2026/06/your-google-business-profile-is-now-ais-primary-source/) (secondary report; exact Google product-naming/date should be verified against Google's own blog)
- Google discontinued the My Business Q&A API on November 3, 2025, replacing GBP Q&A functionality with Gemini-powered AI answers in Google Maps — [cited via WebSearch synthesis, secondary sources, specific primary Google announcement not independently fetched]
- Claims that "ChatGPT's local recommendations increasingly track what's on a business's Google Business Profile" and that this has been "confirmed" appear in agency blogs without naming the confirming primary source — [coremediaconcepts.com](https://coremediaconcepts.com/?p=23242), [texta.ai](https://www.texta.ai/blog/google-business-profile-ai-optimization-guide) — **unverified, treat with caution**
- A Trustpilot analysis (as reported secondhand) of 800,000 AI-generated answers found businesses with little/no review presence rarely appeared in AI-generated recommendations at all — [reported via sociusmarketing.com context; original Trustpilot study not independently located/verified]

### Inferences
- GBP almost certainly helps AI visibility *indirectly* — it feeds Google Maps/Search results and review aggregation, which in turn get crawled/cited by ChatGPT Search and Perplexity as "business website/listing" sources — rather than via a confirmed direct data-sharing pipeline with OpenAI.
- For a production company or artist whose business is not local-service-oriented (not a "find me a plumber nearby" query pattern), GBP is likely a secondary lever rather than primary; it matters more if the entity has a physical studio/office that people search for locally.

### Gaps
- No official OpenAI statement found that names Google Business Profile (or any directory) as a confirmed input into ChatGPT Search's retrieval or ranking.
- No official Google statement found explicitly confirming data-sharing arrangements between Google Business Profile and third-party AI assistants.

## Known "verified AI profile" / knowledge panel offerings to prioritize

### Takeaway
There is no dedicated "verified AI assistant profile" product from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Perplexity as of late 2025/2026 analogous to a business directory listing. The closest real, actionable levers are: Google Knowledge Panel claim/verification, Wikidata entity creation (lower bar than Wikipedia), Wikipedia article (higher bar, strongest long-term signal), and for musicians specifically, a MusicBrainz entry. Bing/Copilot's equivalent mechanism is less documented in available sources.

### Cited Findings
- Google Knowledge Panel claim flow (per multiple third-party guides, not Google's own documentation directly fetched): find the panel, use the "..." menu, click "Claim this knowledge panel," then "GET VERIFIED"; verification is completed by a Google representative and can take time; claimed panels get priority queue treatment for suggested edits — [blitzmetrics.com](https://blitzmetrics.com/how-to-get-a-google-knowledge-panel)
- Some entities have no visible "claim" button if Google hasn't associated the panel with an official entity profile; one guide recommends checking related searches to find the Knowledge Graph ID — [blitzmetrics.com](https://blitzmetrics.com/how-to-unlock-claim-and-merge-google-knowledge-panels/)
- Wikidata has a materially lower bar than Wikipedia: items can exist for people/entities that don't yet meet Wikipedia's notability standard, and Wikidata accepts items referenced by external databases — [kalicube.com](https://kalicube.com/learning-spaces/faq-list/generative-ai/the-wiki-ecosystem-notability-layers-and-the-niche-authority-revolution/)
- For musicians specifically: recommended steps are a MusicBrainz entry, a Wikidata item linking the artist's name to "musician" as an occupation via Wikidata properties, and claiming the Google Knowledge Panel once it appears; Google is described as generating a panel "when enough independent, consistent, machine-readable sources describe the same artist in compatible ways" — [instantpress.co](https://www.instantpress.co/blog/google-knowledge-panel-for-musicians) (vendor source, directionally useful but not an official Google statement)
- One SEO source claims ~58% of Knowledge Panel data points trace back to Wikidata properties (citing a 2023 analysis) — **unverified**, could not confirm original source
- A Wikidata entry does not guarantee a Knowledge Panel will appear, but is described across multiple sources as one of the strongest available signals — [multiple sources, consistent directional claim]

### Inferences
- Priority order for a public figure/artist is likely: (1) Wikidata item (achievable, structured, machine-readable), (2) MusicBrainz entry for a musician specifically, (3) Wikipedia article (harder, but highest-authority long-term), (4) claim the Google Knowledge Panel once/if it appears automatically — rather than trying to "apply" for a panel directly, since panels are algorithmically generated, not requested from scratch.
- No equivalent "claim your AI profile" feature exists yet for ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity individually — visibility on those platforms is earned through the general content/PR/entity-consistency tactics above, not through a dedicated claim/verification portal.

### Gaps
- No documentation found on a Bing/Copilot-specific "knowledge panel" claim process distinct from Bing Places for Business (which is business/local-oriented, not person/artist-oriented).
- Could not verify Google's own official 2025/2026 Knowledge Panel help documentation directly (search results returned third-party guides, not Google's help pages, within the tool-call budget).

## Practical steps for a musician (public figure) and a production company

### Takeaway
Combine (a) entity-foundation work (Wikidata/Wikipedia, schema, sameAs consistency), (b) sustained earned-media/PR output (the strongest evidenced lever), and (c) ongoing monitoring/testing of actual AI assistant outputs — since no single "set and forget" tactic is proven, this is a continuous program, not a one-time project.

### Cited Findings
- Recommended Organization-schema fields for a company: legal name, founding year, founders, headquarters, logo, official profile URLs (`sameAs`) — [winstondigitalmarketing.com](https://www.winstondigitalmarketing.com/playbooks/entity-seo-build-your-brand-entity/)
- Recommended press-page schema: `NewsArticle` on press pages with publisher Organization markup nested under the author; quoted executives get `Person` schema linked back to the Organization; press kits should include `ImageObject`/`VideoObject` markup with dimensions/captions for logos and media assets — [found via WebSearch synthesis of PR/newsroom schema guides]
- Recommended artist-specific schema: `MusicGroup`, `MusicAlbum`, `MusicEvent` markup so AI systems categorize the artist and output correctly — [orphiq.com](https://orphiq.com/resources/artist-entity-authority-ai-seo)
- Recommended measurement practice: repeatedly test target queries against ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini monthly to track whether/how the brand is mentioned, rather than relying solely on traditional web-traffic analytics — [found via WebSearch synthesis, multiple sources agree]
- A `sameAs`/entity-consistency audit (list every external profile that mentions the entity; check for name/logo/founding-date/category conflicts) is a commonly recommended practical exercise — [geo.tenten.co](https://geo.tenten.co/en/blog/brand-entity-consistency-checklist)

### Numbered Action Steps — Musician / Public Figure
1. Create or improve a Wikidata item: name, occupation ("musician"/"singer"), nationality, instance-of, and `sameAs`-equivalent identifiers linking to official site, Spotify/Apple Music artist pages, Instagram, YouTube, MusicBrainz.
2. Create a MusicBrainz entry (artist, releases, credits) if one doesn't exist — it's a standard machine-readable source AI/metadata systems reference.
3. Pursue or strengthen Arabic and English Wikipedia coverage in parallel; Arabic content should use Modern Standard Arabic to maximize regional reach, since Arabic Wikipedia coverage is generally thinner than English and this is a documented structural gap for MENA entities.
4. Add `Person` (and where relevant `MusicGroup`) JSON-LD schema to the artist's official website homepage and bio page, matching Wikidata facts exactly (name spelling, birthdate, nationality) to avoid entity-merging confusion.
5. Build a `sameAs` list on the website's schema pointing to every verified official profile (Wikidata, official site, verified social accounts, streaming-platform artist pages).
6. Invest in earned media over paid promotion: pursue interviews, features, and "best of" list inclusions in Arabic and English outlets — earned-media mentions correlate more strongly with AI citation/visibility than backlinks or schema alone per the Ahrefs/Muck Rack data above.
7. Publish an FAQ-structured bio page answering common factual questions directly ("Who is [artist]?", "What are [artist]'s most famous songs?", "When did [artist] start their career?") in both Arabic and English, since answer-first/FAQ content is repeatedly recommended for AEO.
8. Monitor presence monthly by directly querying ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude with likely fan/press questions about the artist, and log/correct any inaccuracies found (e.g., via official statements, press corrections, or updates to Wikidata/Wikipedia where permitted).
9. Keep NAP-equivalent facts (official name spelling, representation/management contact, official social handles) identical everywhere they appear online, since inconsistency is cited as a cause of AI entity confusion.
10. Treat `llms.txt` as optional/low-priority — implement only if resources allow, without expecting measurable citation gains given current evidence.

### Numbered Action Steps — Production Company
1. Build a complete `Organization` schema on the homepage: legal name, founding year/founders, HQ address, logo, official contact, and `sameAs` links to LinkedIn, Crunchbase (if applicable), official social accounts, and industry-body listings (e.g., local Saudi/GCC media/production registries).
2. Stand up or upgrade a dedicated newsroom/press page using `NewsArticle` schema for releases and `Person` schema (linked to the Organization) for quoted executives/founders.
3. Create a structured press kit (company overview, leadership bios, logo pack, project/portfolio highlights) with `ImageObject`/`VideoObject` schema including captions and dimensions.
4. Pursue a Wikidata item for the company (lower bar than Wikipedia) listing instance-of ("production company"/"media company"), founding date, founders, notable productions, and official website/social `sameAs` links.
5. Pursue Arabic-language earned media coverage specifically (regional trade press, entertainment/media outlets) in MSA to close the documented Arabic AI-visibility gap, in addition to English-language coverage for broader international AI assistant reach.
6. Publish case studies/portfolio pages with clear "answer-first" structure (what the company does, notable clients/productions, founding story) so content is easily extractable for direct-answer AI responses.
7. Maintain a Google Business Profile with accurate category, hours, and service descriptions if the company has a physical office people search for locally — this feeds Google's own Gemini-powered local AI features (Ask Maps) directly, even though its link to ChatGPT specifically is unconfirmed.
8. Run monthly test queries across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude for "who is [company]," "what does [company] produce," and competitor-comparison queries; track citation accuracy and correct any factual drift via updated press releases, website content, or Wikidata edits.
9. Prioritize earned-media/digital-PR output (press releases distributed via recognized wire services, trade-press features) over raw backlink acquisition, since mention-based signals correlate more strongly with AI visibility than backlinks per the Ahrefs study cited above.
10. Audit and fix any NAP/entity inconsistencies (company name variants, old addresses, outdated leadership names) across all platforms where the company is listed, since mismatches are cited as a leading cause of AI "entity confusion."
11. Treat `llms.txt` as optional; it is not proven to affect citation outcomes as of current data (97% of files monitored received zero AI requests per Ahrefs).

### Gaps
- No source directly evaluated the specific interplay of "ChatGPT Business Hub"-type features (platform-specific business profile/plugin features) versus the general tactics above; this research did not find documentation on an OpenAI "Business Hub" product specifically, so this should be flagged to the report writer/user as a term that may not match a documented, named OpenAI feature as of the knowledge cutoff of sources gathered here (Nov 2025-2026).

## Arabic-language / MENA-specific considerations (consolidated)

### Takeaway
MENA/Arabic AI visibility is a known, discussed pain point (thinner Arabic content coverage, weaker AI indexing of Arabic sources) but lacks rigorous independent quantification; practical mitigation centers on MSA content, Arabic Wikipedia/Wikidata investment, and bilingual coverage.

### Cited Findings
- "Near-invisibility of Arabic websites and brands in AI-generated responses" is described as a key MENA problem by regional AI/SEO commentary, though without large-scale independent data — [synthesized from WebSearch, multiple MENA-focused vendor sources, e.g. ai-in-arabia-v2.pages.dev, cntxt.tech]
- Arabic content is described as having weaker AI indexing than English content generally — [same cluster of sources]
- MSA (Modern Standard Arabic) is understood across GCC nationalities and is the register used in formal publishing and by Arabic Wikipedia, making it the recommended register for broad-reach Arabic content aimed at AI visibility — [synthesized from WebSearch, MENA GEO vendor sources]
- A startup, "Zaher AI" (Egypt-based), reportedly raised $150,000 specifically to advance GEO in the MENA region, signaling nascent but growing specialized market interest in this problem — [en.incarabia.com](https://en.incarabia.com/egyptbased-zaher-ai-raises-us150000-to-advance-generative-engine-optimization-in-the-mena-772121.html)
- Google launched an AI-powered Arabic-language search mode across MENA — [menastartupdigest.com](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=18585) (date/specifics not independently verified beyond the secondary report)

### Inferences
- Given the documented gap, a Saudi/Arabic entity should not rely on Arabic content alone or English content alone — maintaining parallel, high-quality MSA and English coverage (press, Wikipedia, website) is likely necessary to be represented accurately by AI assistants in both Arabic and English queries.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or platform-official study quantifying the Arabic AI-visibility gap with specific measured percentages was found; all claims are from secondary/vendor commentary.
- Could not determine whether ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity apply any region-specific logic (e.g., GCC IP-based localization) to how they source or weight Arabic content — no official statement found.
