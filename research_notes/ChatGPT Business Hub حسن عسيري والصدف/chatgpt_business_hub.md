# ChatGPT Business Hub (OpenAI "Businesses in ChatGPT")

**Note on naming (read first):** "ChatGPT Business Hub" is **not** the official product name. As of mid-November 2026, the real, officially-confirmed OpenAI feature is called **"Businesses in ChatGPT"** (also referred to in OpenAI's own help docs as "setting up your business on ChatGPT"), launched in **beta**. Tech press (Search Engine Roundtable, PPC Land, Digiday) used working titles like "Business Hub" and "Claim your business in ChatGPT" while reporting on it pre-launch; the likely URL is `chatgpt.com/business-hub`. Separately, **"ChatGPT Business" is already the name of OpenAI's existing workspace/subscription product** (team seats, admin console) — this is a *different, older* product and a common source of confusion. Keep these two apart in the final report.

## Is "ChatGPT Business Hub" an official, named OpenAI product? What is it officially called?

### Takeaway
No product is officially named "ChatGPT Business Hub." The real, now-launched (beta, as of Nov 10, 2026) OpenAI feature is officially called **"Businesses in ChatGPT."** It is distinct from the pre-existing "ChatGPT Business" subscription tier (team/workspace plan), which has existed since 2024-2025 and is unrelated to business listings/profiles.

## What functionality does it provide (listing, structured data, verification, control over answers)?

### Takeaway
The beta gives verified businesses: a claimable profile/listing, the ability to edit/update business and product info, analytics (mentions/impressions, profile views, website clicks), "discovery topics" (themes in conversations that cite the business), and an optional "Business Agent" that can answer customer questions using business-supplied info. It explicitly does NOT show raw user prompts, individual user data, or competitive rankings.

### Cited Findings
- Core features per OpenAI's beta (as reported): (1) claim and manage business information; (2) view analytics on mentions, profile views, and website clicks; (3) explore "discovery topics" — common conversation themes where the business's website is cited; (4) add a "Business Agent" that lets customers ask follow-up questions about the business's offerings — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-business-hub-42505.html) (Nov 10, 2026)
- A UI tooltip states: "Clicks don't confirm website visits or conversions" — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-business-hub-42505.html)
- Earlier (pre-launch) reporting described the same three core functions via a leaked banner: tracking how often a business is mentioned; adding products/keeping business info current; and a "business agent" answering customer questions using business-supplied context — [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/); [SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-tools-for-business-42476.html)
- Metrics tracked daily and shown as a chart: impressions (how often the business appears in ChatGPT responses) and clicks (to the business's website) — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/chatgpt-is-about-to-show-brands-their-mention-count/); [SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-tools-for-business-42476.html)
- Explicitly excluded from the dashboard: prompt-level data, individual user data, and competitor benchmarking/rankings — [SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-tools-for-business-42476.html); [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/)
- Kaziukėnas (quoted via Digiday) noted the dashboard can't explain *why* mentions/clicks changed — it would need to be paired with third-party AI-visibility/prompt-tracking tools (i.e., third-party GEO tools) for deeper analysis — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/chatgpt-is-about-to-show-brands-their-mention-count/)
- The feature/dashboard is reported as **separate from ChatGPT's advertising product** (Sponsored/paid placements), and aimed at organic/unpaid answer appearances; link between the "Business Agent" and OpenAI's "Sponsored Agents" paid ad format (testing with select US advertisers since Sept 16, 2026) has not been confirmed — [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/); [SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-tools-for-business-42476.html)
- Context: OpenAI's ChatGPT ads (a related but distinct monetization effort) have been in US testing since Feb 2026, reportedly reached a ~$1B annualized run rate by September 2026, and over 100,000 advertisers across 89 countries by late October 2026 — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/chatgpt-is-about-to-show-brands-their-mention-count/)
- Separately, OpenAI has licensed Yelp data (reviews, ratings, photos, business info) so ChatGPT can answer local-business questions, and ChatGPT has gained "local knowledge panels" — [WebSearch result summary citing PinMeto/SERoundtable reporting] — [SERoundtable "ChatGPT Gains Local Knowledge Panels"](https://www.seroundtable.com/openai-adds-local-knowledge-panel-to-chatgpt-results-40635.html) — note: this is a related but distinct feature (third-party licensed data for local search answers), not the Business Hub profile-claiming feature itself.

### Inferences
- The "Business Agent" function is functionally the closest analogue to a Google Business Profile's "direct messaging"/Q&A feature, letting a business control some of what customers see when asking about it in ChatGPT — but it operates on business-supplied info rather than giving full control over all ChatGPT-generated statements about the business.
- Because competitor rankings and prompt-level data are excluded, the tool is positioned as a transparency/analytics + content-management feature, not a "SEO ranking" or "ads auction" control panel — a deliberate design choice likely to avoid the complexity (and liability) of showing businesses exactly how/why they're mentioned or not.

### Gaps
- No detail found on exactly how "mentions" are detected/attributed (i.e., name-entity matching methodology) — one analyst (Kaziukėnas, via RelevantAudience) explicitly flagged this as an open and somewhat technically unclear question: "To count 'mentions of your business', ChatGPT has to know that a name in its answer is your business."
- No official OpenAI documentation text was independently retrieved (403 errors on direct fetch); all functional details are second-hand via Search Engine Roundtable's description of the OpenAI page/help doc.

## Does it support individual/personal brands (public figures, artists, celebrities) or only registered businesses?

### Takeaway
Based on available reporting, the beta is scoped to **registered businesses** (specifically e-commerce and online-service businesses), not individual/personal brands or public figures. No evidence was found of a path for celebrities, artists, or public figures to claim a "profile" the way a business can.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI's own eligibility language (per Search Engine Roundtable's quote of the OpenAI page): the beta is "starting with eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the U.S." — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-business-hub-42505.html) (Nov 10, 2026)
- Verification path described involves a business phone number, DNS verification, or connecting a Shopify store — all businesses/commerce-oriented identity proofs, not individual-identity proofs — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-business-hub-42505.html); [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/)
- No source found describing an individual/personal-brand or public-figure claiming mechanism in ChatGPT analogous to this Business Hub.
- Separately, independent research exists on *how often* ChatGPT and other AI engines mention celebrity-founded brands (e.g., Rare Beauty, Fenty Beauty) and on accuracy problems (one study found 18% of AI-recommended celebrity-brand partnerships were fabricated) — but this is about measuring AI output, not about a claimable personal profile — [5WPR research, via WebSearch summary](https://www.5wpr.com/research/celebrity-brand-ai-demand/)

### Inferences
- Given the verification methods (business phone, DNS on a business domain, Shopify store), this product is architecturally built around *commercial entities with a website/domain and phone line*, which would exclude most individual public figures unless they operate through a registered business entity with its own verifiable domain/commerce presence.

### Gaps
- No OpenAI statement found on whether individual/personal-brand support is planned for a future phase.
- Could not confirm whether sole proprietors or solo creators with an online store (vs. "companies") are eligible — eligibility is stated only in the broad categories "ecommerce and online service businesses."

## How does a business sign up / verify / claim a profile, step by step?

### Takeaway
Per the Nov 10, 2026 beta, a business claims its listing via one of three verification paths: (1) text message to the main business number, (2) phone call to the main business number, or (3) DNS verification on the business's domain. The reporting journalist (Barry Schwartz) used the DNS method personally.

### Cited Findings
- "Businesses can claim a listing by text or phone call to the main business number, or through DNS verification. The author used DNS [verification]." — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-business-hub-42505.html) (Nov 10, 2026)
- Earlier (pre-launch) reporting mentioned "connecting a Shopify store" as one way to prove authority to represent a business — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/chatgpt-is-about-to-show-brands-their-mention-count/); [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/)

- Pre-launch reporting said claiming requires proving "authority to represent the business" but did not fully specify all accepted proof methods beyond the Shopify example — [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/)

## How does this compare practically to Google Business Profile?

### Takeaway
Structurally similar (free claimable listing + verification + analytics + ability to edit business info + a Q&A-like feature), but ChatGPT's version is narrower in scope: U.S.-only, ecommerce/online-service businesses only (vs. GBP's near-universal, global, any-business-type eligibility), has no public-facing "profile page," no review/rating system of its own (it instead surfaces licensed third-party reviews, e.g., from Yelp), no competitor benchmarking, and no map/local-search integration comparable to Google Maps.

### Cited Findings
- Analysts/journalists explicitly likened OpenAI's tool to "Google Search Console / Webmaster Tools" (an analytics/ownership-verification tool) rather than to Google Business Profile per se, though the "claim your business" framing and business-info-management function mirror GBP's core purpose — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-tools-for-business-42476.html) (Nov 6, 2026)
- Google Business Profile itself is widely treated by SEO/GEO analysts as a key *input* for how AI engines (including ChatGPT) answer local-business questions — one analysis states ChatGPT has "a stronger basis for inclusion when facts are consistent across a crawlable website, Google Business Profile, directories, review sites, local press, and structured data" — i.e., GBP currently functions as upstream data ChatGPT may draw on, separately from OpenAI's own new first-party claim feature — [general WebSearch synthesis of SEO/GEO vendor commentary, unattributed to single primary source — treat as lower confidence]
- OpenAI separately licensed Yelp's review/rating/photo/business-info data for ChatGPT's local answers — functioning somewhat like a review layer, but it is a third-party data licensing deal, not OpenAI's own native review system — [referenced in SERoundtable's "ChatGPT Gains Local Knowledge Panels" coverage, cited via search synthesis]
- No ranking/competitor-comparison feature exists in the new OpenAI tool (explicitly excluded), whereas Google Business Profile implicitly competes within Google's local-pack/Maps ranking that businesses can observe via their own ranking relative to competitors in search results — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/chatgpt-business-hub-42505.html); [RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/chatgpt-claim-your-business-hub-mention-click-charts/)

### Inferences
- OpenAI's tool is best understood as "Google Business Profile's claim/verify/edit mechanic" fused with "Search Console's analytics mechanic," minus GBP's public map/local-pack presence, review system, and competitor visibility — a narrower, earlier-stage product given it just exited pre-launch testing in Nov 2026.
- Because eligibility is U.S.-only and ecommerce/online-service-only at launch, it is far more restrictive than Google Business Profile, which supports virtually any business type/location globally.

### Gaps
- No independent, named analyst piece doing a formal point-by-point "ChatGPT Business Hub vs Google Business Profile" comparison was found during this research window (as of Nov 12, 2026) — most "comparison" content in search results was vendor blogs about connecting GBP data *into* ChatGPT via third-party plugins/MCP connectors, a different topic (data syndication, not the new native OpenAI feature).
- Given the feature only became confirmed/public on Nov 10, 2026 (two days before this research was conducted), it is very likely that more detailed journalistic comparisons (TechCrunch, The Verge, Search Engine Land, etc.) had not yet been published or indexed at the time of this research.
